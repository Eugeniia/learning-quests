### Generative AI for Beginners
AI - creatin intelligent machines capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence.
ML - branch of AI that teaches computers to learn from data and improve over time without being explicitly programmed.
Deep Learning - subset of ML that uses complex algorithms and neural networks to handle complicated problems.
Generative AI (GenAI) - subset of Deep Learning.
ChatGPT - an AI chatbot developed by OpenAI that uses LLM (e.g. GPT-5). It is designed for natural language understanding and generation, specifically ina  conversational context.

OpenAI (general use), Gemini (for general purposes or automation), Grok, Claude (code generation), Copilot, Perplexity.

LLMs - powerful AI models designed for understanding and generating human-like text.

Prompt Engineering - the process of crafting well-defined and structured input queries to interact with AI systems, in order to get accurate and relevant responses.

Machines DO NOT understand text. They only understand numbers.
Embeddings - numerical representation of text. They are essential for AI models to understand and work with human language effectively.

Fine Tuning a LLM - the process of adapting a pre-trained model to preform specific tasks or to cater to a particular domain more effectively. Self-supervised, supervised and reinforcment i(learning by feedback) learning.

Knowledge Cutt-off Date - AI models are trained only up to a certain date and don't know about recent events or updates. No access to private data - AI cannot answer questions from your internal documents, policies, or proprietary information that wasn't part of its training.
Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) - a technique that enhances AI responses by first retrieving relevant information from a local knowledge base. Retrieval - finding the right facts from your own knowledge source. Augmented - adding those facts to your question. Generation - letting the AI write a response based on that fresh data. How RAG helps: authorative sources, total privacy, reduced hallucinations, sources included.

Agentic AI - AI systems that can autonomously plan, decide, and execute actions to achieve specific goals without constant human intervention. Plan, Autonomous ACtions, LLM (model training, Tools), Memory (short term contextual, long term knowledge base), Tools, Feedback/Iterate.

# Ethical Considerations and Future Trends
Responsible AI - the ethical and moral framework that guides the development, deployment, and use of AI systems to ensure they align with human values and societal norms.
Bias & discrimination, privacy concerns, legal consequences, loss of trust.
Ethical principles, data quality, transparency, consent & compliance, monitor & improve, human in the loop.
Shift to smaller, targeted models. Multimodal AI Fusion, repsonsible & regulated AI. Moving from ideas to implementations.
Skill shift and new job opportunities. More impact on knowledge work. Most impact on customer operations, legal, marketing/sales, software engineering, R&D. Human productivity increase.
